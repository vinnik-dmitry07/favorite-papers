## Summary:

This paper delves into the intriguing question of why large language models (LLMs) struggle with multi-operand addition, a task that, while seemingly simple for humans, presents a significant challenge for these models. The authors introduce the concept of a 'lookahead' heuristic, which they define as the model's tendency to examine only a single preceding digit when performing addition, primarily to anticipate carry propagation. This heuristic, they argue, is effective for two-operand addition but fails when extended to multi-operand scenarios. The core of the paper lies in the authors' empirical investigation of this heuristic. They conduct extensive experiments across various model sizes and tokenization strategies, consistently demonstrating that the identified lookahead heuristic is a key factor in the models' inability to generalize to more complex numerical reasoning tasks. Specifically, the authors probe the internal representations of the LLMs and evaluate their digit-wise accuracy to validate the presence of this heuristic. The paper's findings suggest that the limitations of LLMs in arithmetic are not due to a fundamental lack of numerical understanding, but rather due to the way these models process multi-digit numbers. The authors also explore the impact of different tokenization strategies on the model's ability to perform multi-operand addition, finding that even with multi-digit tokenization, the lookahead heuristic persists. This work is significant because it provides a clear and actionable explanation for a well-documented limitation of LLMs in arithmetic reasoning. It highlights a specific mechanism that contributes to the models' struggles with numerical tasks and provides a foundation for future research aimed at improving LLMs' arithmetic capabilities. The paper's focus on the internal mechanisms of LLMs, particularly the lookahead heuristic, offers valuable insights into the models' limitations and opens up avenues for developing more robust and reliable arithmetic reasoning capabilities in these models. The authors' work is a crucial step towards understanding the inner workings of LLMs and their limitations in numerical tasks, and it provides a solid foundation for future research in this area.
