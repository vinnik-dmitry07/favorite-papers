## Reviewer

### Summary

This paper presents a general model-free RL algorithm that leverages model-based representations to achieve the sample efficiency and performance of model-based methods, without the computational overhead. The proposed approach is based on learning features that approximately capture a linear relationship between state-action pairs and value. The method is evaluated on four widely used RL benchmarks and 118 environments, and achieves competitive performance against state-of-the-art domain-specific and general baselines without algorithmic or hyperparameter changes between environments or benchmarks.

### Strengths

1. The paper is well-written and easy to follow.
2. The proposed method is simple and effective.
3. The experimental results are promising.

### Weaknesses

1. The novelty is limited. The idea of learning a representation that captures a linear relationship between state-action pairs and value is not new, and has been explored in previous works such as TD7 and many other representation learning methods. The main difference is that the proposed method learns the representation using a model-based approach, but the model is not used for planning, which is similar to TD7. 
2. The motivation is not clear. The authors claim that the model-based representation can capture the linear relationship between state-action pairs and value, but it is not clear why this is the case. The authors should provide more theoretical analysis or empirical evidence to support this claim. 
3. The evaluation is limited. The proposed method is evaluated on four widely used RL benchmarks and 118 environments, but it is not clear how the method performs on more complex or challenging tasks. The authors should provide more comprehensive evaluation results to demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed method.

### Questions

1. What is the main difference between the proposed method and TD7? Why is the proposed method more effective than TD7?
2. Can the proposed method be applied to more complex or challenging tasks?
3. How does the proposed method perform on tasks with high-dimensional observations or actions?
