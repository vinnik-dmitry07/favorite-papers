## Summary

This paper reviews the shift from weights to context to harness in LLM agents. It argues that agent infrastructure matters not merely because it adds auxiliary components, but because it transforms hard cognitive burdens into forms that the model can solve more reliably. The paper traces a historical progression from weights to context to harness, analyzes memory, skills, and protocols as three distinct but coupled forms of externalization, and examines how they interact inside a larger agent system. It also discusses the trade-off between parametric and externalized capability, identifies emerging directions such as self-evolving harnesses and shared agent infrastructure, and discusses open challenges in evaluation, governance, and the long-term co-evolution of models and external infrastructure.
