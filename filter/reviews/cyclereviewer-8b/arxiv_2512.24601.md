## Reviewer

### Summary

The paper proposes a new paradigm called Recursive Language Models (RLMs) for processing arbitrarily long prompts. RLMs treat the prompt as part of the environment, allowing the LLM to examine, decompose, and recursively call itself over prompt snippets. The authors demonstrate that RLMs can process inputs beyond the context window limits of frontier LLMs, outperforming vanilla LLMs and long-context methods on four long-context tasks, while maintaining comparable cost. They also show that post-training an RLM can improve its performance.

### Strengths

1. The paper introduces a novel approach to handling long prompts in LLMs, which is an important problem given the increasing adoption of LLMs for long-horizon tasks.

2. The authors provide a comprehensive evaluation of RLMs on four diverse long-context tasks, demonstrating their effectiveness and efficiency.

3. The paper is well-structured and easy to follow.

### Weaknesses

1. The paper lacks a detailed discussion of the limitations of RLMs. While the authors mention some limitations in the appendix, a more thorough discussion in the main paper would provide a more balanced view of the approach.

2. The paper does not provide a clear comparison with other methods for handling long prompts. While the authors mention some related work in the introduction, a more comprehensive comparison with existing methods would help to establish the novelty and effectiveness of RLMs.

3. The paper does not provide a clear explanation of how RLMs are able to outperform vanilla LLMs and long-context methods. While the authors mention some possible reasons in the discussion, a more detailed analysis would help to understand the underlying mechanisms.

### Questions

1. Can the authors provide a more detailed discussion of the limitations of RLMs?
