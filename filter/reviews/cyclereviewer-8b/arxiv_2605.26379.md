### Summary

This paper studies the identifiability of LeJEPA, a type of joint embedding predictive architectures (JEPA), which is a type of self-supervised learning. The authors show that LeJEPA can learn a linearly identifiable representation if and only if the latent variables are Gaussian. The forward direction relies on a spectral decomposition that penalizes every degree of nonlinearity, while the converse rules out every non-Gaussian alternative. The authors further prove that identifiability degrades gracefully when the objectives are only approximately satisfied, and that linear identifiability suffices for optimal latent-space planning. The authors validate their theory with experiments on 2D examples, 1024-dimensional latents, distributional ablations, and pixel-based robotic control.
