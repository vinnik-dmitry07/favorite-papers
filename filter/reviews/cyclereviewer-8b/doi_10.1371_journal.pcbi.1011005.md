### Summary

The authors propose a learning rule for spiking neurons that is inspired by the regression discontinuity design (RDD) approach to causal inference. The idea is that a neuron can estimate its causal effect on the reward signal by comparing the reward when the neuron is just above and just below its firing threshold. This idea is tested in a simple two-neuron network and in a single hidden layer neural network of varying width.

### Strengths

The idea of using the spiking discontinuity to estimate the causal effect of a neuron on the reward signal is interesting and novel. The paper is well written and easy to follow.

### Weaknesses

The main weakness of the paper is that the results are not convincing. The authors use a very simple model to test their idea, and the results are not convincing. For example, in Fig 3A, the authors show that the spiking discontinuity estimator is unbiased when p is small, but this is not surprising. In fact, if p is too small, the estimator will be very noisy. In Fig 3B, the authors show that the linear model is unbiased over larger window sizes, but this is not surprising either. In fact, if p is too large, the estimator will be biased. The authors need to show that their estimator is unbiased for a wider range of p values. Additionally, the authors need to show that their estimator is more accurate than other estimators, such as the naive observed dependence estimator. 

The authors also need to provide more details about the simulations. For example, how many simulations were run? What was the sample size? What was the significance level? How were the results averaged? 

The authors also need to provide more details about the model. For example, how was the reward function chosen? How was the threshold chosen? How was the correlation coefficient chosen? 

The authors also need to provide more details about the learning rule. For example, how was the learning rate chosen? How was the window size chosen? How was the threshold chosen? 

Finally, the authors need to provide more details about the biological relevance of their results. For example, how does this learning rule relate to other learning rules that have been proposed for spiking neurons?
