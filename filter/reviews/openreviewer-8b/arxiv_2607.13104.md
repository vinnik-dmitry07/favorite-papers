# Review

## Summary
This paper provides a comprehensive survey of self-improvement in modern AI, focusing on foundation models and agentic systems. It introduces a system-level framework that couples foundation models with operational scaffolds and formalizes self-improvement as a self-induced update operator. The authors organize prior work by update target and the signals driving change, reviewing applications and discussing evaluation methods. The paper concludes by identifying open problems and future directions in the field.

## Strengths
The paper provides a clear and well-structured overview of the evolution of self-improvement in AI, from classical AI to modern foundation models. It effectively organizes the field around a unified taxonomy that distinguishes between foundation model improvement and scaffold improvement. The paper also thoroughly reviews existing methods, their applications, and current evaluation protocols, highlighting the empirical landscape and the need for reliable self-improvement mechanisms.

## Weaknesses
1. The paper could benefit from a more detailed discussion on the potential risks and challenges associated with self-improvement in AI, such as the risk of unintended consequences or the potential for bias amplification. This would provide a more balanced view of the field.
2. The paper could further enhance its depth by including a more detailed discussion on the ethical implications of self-improvement in AI, such as the potential impact on employment or the need for new regulatory frameworks.

## Questions
1. How do the authors envision addressing the potential risks associated with self-improvement in AI, such as the risk of unintended consequences or the potential for bias amplification?
2. Could the authors elaborate on the ethical implications of self-improvement in AI, particularly in terms of the potential impact on employment and the need for new regulatory frameworks?
