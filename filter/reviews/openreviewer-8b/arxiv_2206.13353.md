# Review

## Summary
This paper lays out a six-premise argument for existential risk from power-seeking AI by 2070. The premises are: (1) it will become possible and financially feasible to build relevantly powerful and agentic AI systems; (2) there will be strong incentives to do so; (3) it will be much harder to build aligned (and relevantly powerful/agentic) AI systems than to build misaligned (and relevantly powerful/agentic) AI systems that are still superficially attractive to deploy; (4) some such misaligned systems will seek power over humans in high-impact ways; (5) this problem will scale to the full disempowerment of humanity; and (6) such disempowerment will constitute an existential catastrophe. The author assigns rough subjective credences to the premises in this argument, and ends up with an overall estimate of ~5% that an existential catastrophe of this kind will occur by 2070.

## Strengths
The paper is well written and clear. It lays out the argument in a way that is easy to follow and understand. The arguments are well thought out and well justified. The topic is extremely important, and the paper does a good job of breaking down the argument for existential risk into its constituent parts, and of addressing potential counterarguments.

## Weaknesses
I do not see any major weaknesses in the paper. I think it would be good to add a brief discussion of the probability of human extinction, and of the probability of human extinction from AI, based on current knowledge. This would give readers who are not familiar with the literature a better sense of the scale of the risk. It would also be good to include a brief discussion of the probability of human extinction from other causes, such as climate change, nuclear war, etc. This would help put the AI-related risk into perspective.
