# Review

## Summary
This is a textbook on reinforcement learning. It covers the basics of RL, including MDPs, basic algorithms such as value iteration and policy iteration, and some common techniques such as Q-learning and policy gradients. It also covers some extensions such as continuous action spaces and model-based RL. Finally, it covers some more advanced topics such as multi-task RL and multi-agent RL.

## Strengths
The presentation is clear and easy to follow. The authors have done a good job of explaining complex concepts in an understandable way. The figures and tables are helpful in illustrating key points. The authors have also provided code to reproduce the results.

## Weaknesses
The authors claim that the main contribution of this work is to explain why the algorithms look the way they are and not otherwise. However, I don't see this contribution in the paper. The paper is more of a survey of existing RL algorithms and their theoretical justifications, rather than a novel contribution. The authors should clarify their contributions and be more explicit about what is new in their work.

The authors should also discuss the limitations of their work. For example, the focus of the paper is on RL algorithms, but the authors do not discuss the impact of function approximation on these algorithms. This is an important topic, as function approximation is widely used in practical RL applications. The authors should address this gap in their discussion.

Finally, the authors should provide some intuition for why the algorithms were developed in their work. What problems were they designed to solve? What were the trade-offs made? How do these algorithms compare to each other? These are all important questions that the authors should address in their work.
