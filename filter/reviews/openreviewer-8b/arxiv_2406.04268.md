# Review

## Summary
The paper argues that open-endedness is a necessary component of AGI, and that the current generation of foundation models are not open-ended. The paper provides a formal definition of open-endedness, and illustrates what open-endedness would look like in a few different contexts (AlphaGo, AdA, POET, and foundation models). The paper then discusses four paths towards open-ended foundation models: reinforcement learning, self-improvement, task generation, and evolutionary algorithms. Finally, the paper discusses the importance of safety considerations in the development of open-ended foundation models.

## Strengths
The paper is well-written and clear. It provides a clear definition of open-endedness and does a good job of illustrating what open-endedness would look like in a few different contexts. The discussion of the four paths towards open-ended foundation models is also useful. I especially appreciated the discussion of safety considerations for open-ended foundation models.

## Weaknesses
The paper is not particularly novel, and it does not provide any new concrete proposals for achieving open-endedness. The definition of open-endedness is also a bit vague, and it is not clear to me that it is formal enough to be useful.

## Questions
- The definition of open-endedness is not quite formal enough to be useful. In particular, the definition of learnability seems a bit vague. I would appreciate if the authors could provide a more formal definition of learnability, perhaps in terms of a specific model class or a specific learning algorithm. This would help to clarify the definition of open-endedness and make it more useful for guiding research.

- The authors argue that foundation models are not open-ended, but it is not clear to me why this is the case. It seems to me that foundation models could be open-ended if the training distribution is not fixed, but instead is continually updated with new data generated by the model itself. In particular, it seems to me that foundation models could be open-ended if they are trained on a distribution that is continually shifted towards more novel and challenging examples, as judged by some measure of novelty and difficulty. This would provide a clear path towards open-endedness, and would address the issue of the fixed training distribution that the authors argue is a key limitation of current foundation models. I would appreciate if the authors could comment on this.
