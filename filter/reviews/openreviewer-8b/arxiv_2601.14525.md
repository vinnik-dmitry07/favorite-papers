# Review

## Summary
This paper presents a system for automatically executing LLM-generated ideas and a study of using this system to perform evolutionary search and RL finetuning on LLMs to improve their idea generation capabilities. The authors find that their execution system is able to execute a large fraction of ideas generated by LLMs, and that evolutionary search is more effective than best-of-N sampling for taking advantage of this system. They also find that RL can improve the average performance of LLMs on this task, but does not lead to any improvement in the best performance.

## Strengths
- The paper is well-written and easy to follow. The authors do a good job of explaining the setup and results.
- The paper presents a novel system for executing LLM-generated code, which could be of independent interest.
- The paper presents a novel study of using execution-based feedback to improve LLMs' idea generation capabilities, including the novel finding that RL leads to a collapse in thinking length and idea diversity.

## Weaknesses
- The paper only considers two tasks (pre-training and post-training), and it's not clear how well the results will generalize to other tasks.
- The paper does not compare to other methods for improving LLMs with execution feedback, such as those mentioned in the related work section.

## Questions
- How do the results depend on the choice of baseline codebase? In particular, how would things change if the baseline were much better (e.g. if someone else had already found the optimal configuration for the tasks considered)?
- Do you have any thoughts on why RL does not improve the max reward?
