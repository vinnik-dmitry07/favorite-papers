# Review

## Summary
The paper studies the interaction of dislocations with platelet linear complexions in Al-Cu alloys. The paper shows that the interaction results in faceting of the dislocations, with the dislocations climbing up the platelets. The paper shows that the dislocations have to move back down the platelets to continue their motion. This is different from the usual Orowan looping mechanism. The paper derives a modified Orowan equation to describe the critical resolved shear stress as a function of platelet size and spacing.

## Strengths
The interaction of dislocations with linear complexions is an interesting topic. The paper shows that the interaction results in a dislocation climb mechanism that is different from the usual Orowan looping mechanism. The paper derives a modified Orowan equation to describe the critical resolved shear stress as a function of platelet size and spacing. This equation is interesting as it shows that the strength is inversely proportional to platelet spacing, and depends on the size of the platelets. The paper is well written.

## Weaknesses
The paper only studies one configuration of platelet linear complexions. It would be interesting to study how the dislocation interaction changes as a function of the platelet orientation, size, and spacing. The paper would also benefit from a more detailed comparison with the Orowan looping mechanism. It is not clear how the critical resolved shear stress in the present work compares to the critical resolved shear stress for the Orowan looping mechanism.

## Questions
1. The paper would benefit from a more detailed comparison with the Orowan looping mechanism. It is not clear how the critical resolved shear stress in the present work compares to the critical resolved shear stress for the Orowan looping mechanism.
2. How does the dislocation interaction change as a function of the platelet orientation, size, and spacing?
3. The paper mentions that the modified strengthening law (equation 1) is similar to the Orowan equation, but varies in a number of important ways. It would be useful to show how the critical resolved shear stress from equation 1 compares to the critical resolved shear stress from the Orowan equation for the same platelet configuration.
4. The paper mentions that the platelet complexions are also inclined with respect to the dislocation line length, and can be thicker in the out-of-plane direction near the dislocation line. It would be useful to show how this inclination and thickness affects the dislocation interaction.
5. The paper mentions that the platelet array linear complexions create a complex, non-planar configuration out of a once simple partial dislocation pair. It would be useful to show how this non-planar configuration affects the dislocation motion compared to a planar configuration.
