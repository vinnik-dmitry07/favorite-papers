# Review

## Summary
This paper proposes Self-Refine, an approach for improving initial outputs from LLMs through iterative feedback and refinement. The method does not require any additional training, and uses a single LLM as the generator, refiner and the feedback provider. The authors evaluate Self-Refine on 7 diverse tasks, ranging from dialog response generation to mathematical reasoning, using state-of-the-art LLMs. The results show that Self-Refine can improve the performance of LLMs on these tasks.

## Strengths
1. The paper is well-written and easy to follow.
2. The idea of Self-Refine is simple and effective. It does not require any additional training, and can be applied to various tasks without requiring extensive supervision.
3. The authors evaluate Self-Refine on a diverse set of tasks, and the results show that it can improve the performance of state-of-the-art LLMs on these tasks.

## Weaknesses
1. The paper does not provide a detailed analysis of the computational cost of Self-Refine. It would be helpful to compare the computational cost of Self-Refine to the computational cost of other approaches that improve LLM performance, such as fine-tuning and reinforcement learning.
2. The paper does not provide a detailed analysis of the limitations of Self-Refine. It would be helpful to discuss any potential drawbacks or scenarios where Self-Refine may not be effective.

## Questions
1. How does the computational cost of Self-Refine compare to other approaches that improve LLM performance, such as fine-tuning and reinforcement learning?
2. What are the limitations of Self-Refine, and are there scenarios where it may not be effective?
