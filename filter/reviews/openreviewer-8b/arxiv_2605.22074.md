# Review

## Summary
This paper proposes a novel approach for training large language models on mathematical reasoning tasks using reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR). The method, called Subproblem Curriculum Reinforcement Learning (SCRL), addresses the challenge of credit assignment in RLVR by breaking down complex problems into a sequence of verifiable subproblems. This curriculum learning structure allows the model to receive finer-grained feedback and improve its reasoning capabilities.

## Strengths
1. The paper presents a creative solution to the credit assignment problem in RLVR by using subproblem decomposition. This approach is innovative and shows promise in improving the training of LLMs for reasoning tasks.

2. The paper is well-structured and clearly written. The authors provide a thorough explanation of the SCRL framework, including how subproblems are generated and how credit is assigned at the subproblem level.

3. The theoretical analysis provided in the paper adds depth to the understanding of how subproblem decomposition can lift hard problems out of gradient dead zones. This analysis strengthens the overall argument for the effectiveness of SCRL.

## Weaknesses
1. The paper could provide more details on how the subproblems are generated and the quality of these subproblems is not well discussed. 

2. The paper could benefit from a more extensive discussion of the limitations of the proposed method. For example, it would be helpful to address potential challenges in the subproblem generation process or scenarios where SCRL may not be as effective.

## Questions
1. How does the quality of subproblems affect the performance of SCRL? Have the authors explored the impact of using subproblems generated by different models or with varying levels of difficulty?

2. How does SCRL compare to other state-of-the-art methods for training LLMs on mathematical reasoning tasks, such as those using alternative curriculum learning approaches or different RL algorithms?

3. Are there any potential limitations or drawbacks of SCRL that the authors have identified during the course of this research? How might these be addressed in future work?
