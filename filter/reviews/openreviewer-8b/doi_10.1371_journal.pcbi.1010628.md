# Review

## Summary
The authors use a spiking neural network to study how the brain might prevent catastrophic forgetting by replaying previously learned tasks during periods of sleep. They show that the network can learn two tasks, but that sequential training results in catastrophic forgetting. However, if they interleave training with periods of unsupervised learning (simulating sleep), then catastrophic forgetting is prevented.

## Strengths
The paper is well written and the experiments are well thought out and explained. The results are interesting and relevant to the catastrophic forgetting literature.

## Weaknesses
The authors could do more to relate their work to the existing literature on sleep and memory consolidation. For example, the authors could discuss the role of the different stages of sleep (REM and NREM) and how they might play a different role in memory processing. The authors could also relate their work to the literature on memory reactivation and the role of the hippocampus in reactivating memories and updating them into long term storage (for example, see the work of Frankland and colleagues, such as: Frankland, P. W., & Gallagher, M. (2001). Hippocampal neurogenesis and the regulation of memory consolidation. Nature neuroscience, 4(2), 137-142).
