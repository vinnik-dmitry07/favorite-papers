# Review

## Summary
The paper presents a theoretical result that shows that any agent that learns well under distributional shifts must have learned an approximate causal model of the data generating process. The result is presented for a specific class of causal models (causal Bayesian networks) and a specific class of distributional shifts (local interventions).

## Strengths
- The paper is well-written and easy to follow.
- The paper addresses an important and relevant question: what capabilities are necessary for general intelligence.
- The paper presents a novel theoretical result with interesting implications.

## Weaknesses
- The result is presented for a very specific class of causal models (causal Bayesian networks) and a specific class of distributional shifts (local interventions). It is not clear whether the result can be extended to more general causal models and distributional shifts.
- The paper does not provide any empirical evidence to support the theoretical results.

## Questions
- Can the result be extended to more general causal models and/or distributional shifts?
- What are the implications of the result for the development of AI systems? How can the result guide the design and development of more intelligent systems?
- How does the result relate to existing theories and frameworks for general intelligence? What are the implications for theories such as the theory of universal intelligence by Legg and Hutter?
