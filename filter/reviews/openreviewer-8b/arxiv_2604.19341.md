# Review

## Summary
This paper introduces a new framework called SimpleTES, which is an evaluation-driven scaling framework for AI-driven scientific discovery. The key idea is to structure the scaling of AI-driven discovery loops to improve the efficiency and effectiveness of finding novel solutions to open-ended problems. The authors demonstrate the effectiveness of SimpleTES by applying it to 28 open-ended problems across diverse scientific domains, such as quantum physics, astronomy, biology, AI, and mathematics. The results show that SimpleTES can achieve state-of-the-art solutions in these domains using a single open-source GPT-OSS model, outperforming human experts and other AI systems that use larger proprietary models. The paper also explores how to post-train the model for long-horizon discovery, which improves the performance on both training and held-out mathematics problems.

## Strengths
- The paper introduces a novel framework, SimpleTES, that offers a structured approach to scaling AI-driven scientific discovery.
- The authors provide a comprehensive evaluation of SimpleTES on 28 open-ended problems across diverse scientific domains, demonstrating its effectiveness and versatility.
- The paper explores the impact of post-training the model on its own successful trajectories, which improves the performance on long-horizon discovery problems.
- The authors discuss the broader impact of their work and the potential for future research directions, including extending the framework to more complex scientific discovery tasks and integrating richer semantic feedback.

## Weaknesses
- The paper could benefit from a more detailed comparison with existing AI discovery systems, such as AlphaEvolve, in terms of performance and efficiency.
- While the paper demonstrates the effectiveness of SimpleTES on a variety of problems, it would be helpful to see how it performs on more complex and challenging scientific discovery tasks.
- The paper could provide more insights into the limitations of SimpleTES, such as its dependence on the quality and reliability of the evaluators.

## Questions
- How does SimpleTES compare to other AI discovery systems in terms of computational resources and time required to achieve state-of-the-art solutions?
- Can SimpleTES be applied to more complex scientific discovery tasks, such as drug discovery or material design, and what would be the challenges involved?
- How does the post-training of the model on its own successful trajectories affect the overall performance and creativity of the model in discovering novel solutions?
- What are the potential limitations of SimpleTES, and how can they be addressed in future research?
