# Review

## Summary
The paper investigates whether LLMs possess metacognitive abilities by introducing a neuroscience-inspired neurofeedback paradigm. The authors examine LLMs' capacity to monitor and control their internal activations using in-context learning. The study reveals that LLMs can effectively monitor and control a limited subset of their neural activations, influenced by factors like the number of in-context examples and the semantic interpretability of the neural direction. This work provides empirical evidence for quantifying metacognition in LLMs, with significant implications for AI safety and potential applications in adversarial attack and defense mechanisms.

## Strengths
- The paper is well written and easy to follow. The figures are also well made and informative.
- The paper addresses a very interesting question of whether LLMs can monitor and control their internal activations.
- The experiments are well thought out and comprehensive. The authors examine multiple different axes, datasets, and models.

## Weaknesses
- The paper only considers binary classification tasks. It would be interesting to see if the results hold for open-ended generation tasks.

## Questions
- Do you expect the results to hold for open-ended generation tasks?
- Do you expect the results to hold for other types of tasks (e.g., reasoning, ICL, etc.)?
