# Review

## Summary
This paper introduces Kosmos-1, a multimodal large language model (MLLM) that can perceive general modalities, follow instructions, and perform in-context learning. The model is trained on web-scale multimodal corpora, including text, image-caption pairs, and interleaved image-text data. The authors evaluate Kosmos-1 on a wide range of tasks, including language understanding, perception-language tasks, and vision tasks, without any gradient updates or finetuning. The results show that Kosmos-1 achieves impressive performance on these tasks and demonstrates the benefits of cross-modal transfer, i.e., transferring knowledge from language to multimodal and from multimodal to language. The paper also introduces a dataset of Raven IQ test to diagnose the nonverbal reasoning capability of MLLMs.

## Strengths
1. The paper is well-written and easy to follow.
2. The paper introduces a novel dataset for evaluating the nonverbal reasoning capability of MLLMs, which is a new and important aspect of intelligence.
3. The paper shows that MLLMs can benefit from cross-modal transfer, i.e., transferring knowledge from language to multimodal and from multimodal to language. This is an important finding for the development of MLLMs.

## Weaknesses
1. The paper does not provide a detailed analysis of the limitations of Kosmos-1 or potential challenges in scaling up the model.
2. The paper does not provide a detailed analysis of the impact of the size of the training data on the performance of Kosmos-1.
3. The paper does not provide a detailed analysis of the impact of the model architecture on the performance of Kosmos-1.

## Questions
1. How does the size of the training data affect the performance of Kosmos-1?
2. What are the limitations of Kosmos-1, and how can they be addressed in future work?
