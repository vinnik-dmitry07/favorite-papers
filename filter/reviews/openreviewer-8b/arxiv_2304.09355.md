## Summary
This paper provides a comprehensive overview of the connection between information theory, self-supervised learning, and deep neural networks. The authors examine various self-supervised learning methods through the lens of information theory, introducing a unified framework that encompasses these approaches. The paper delves into the application of the information bottleneck principle, explores connections between information theory and generalization, and investigates recent information-theoretic learning algorithms. Additionally, the authors discuss how an information-theoretic perspective can inform the design of improved self-supervised learning algorithms and the potential benefits of integrating information theory into SSL across diverse applications.
