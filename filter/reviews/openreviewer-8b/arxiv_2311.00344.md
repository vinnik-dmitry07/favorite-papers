# Review

## Summary
This paper presents a formal definition of open-ended learning (OEL) in the context of goal-conditioned agents. The paper first reviews the history of the concept of OEL, and how it has been used in the literature. The paper then proposes a definition of OEL based on the idea that the agent (or the environment) is producing novel goals (or tasks) over time. The paper then discusses how this definition relates to other concepts such as lifelong learning and continual learning, and how it can be used to build agents that can learn a growing repertoire of goal-driven skills.

## Strengths
- The paper provides a clear and concise definition of open-ended learning for goal-conditioned agents.
- The paper reviews the history of the concept of open-ended learning and how it has been used in the literature, which helps to contextualize the current definition.
- The paper discusses how the proposed definition relates to other concepts such as lifelong learning and continual learning.

## Weaknesses
- The paper does not provide any concrete examples or case studies to illustrate the proposed definition of open-ended learning.
- The paper does not provide any empirical results or evaluations of agents that demonstrate the open-ended learning property.
- The paper does not provide any discussion of the limitations of the proposed definition or potential ways to extend it.

## Questions
- How can we evaluate an agent's open-ended learning property in practice, given the finite lifespan of most agents?
- How can we compare the performance of different open-ended learning agents?
- What are some concrete examples of open-ended learning agents in the literature that satisfy the proposed definition?
- How does the proposed definition relate to other concepts such as creativity and innovation in the context of agent design?
