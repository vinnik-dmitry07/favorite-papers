# Review

## Summary
This paper is a philosophical analysis of the question of whether large language models are conscious. It first reviews various possible criteria that have been suggested in the literature for determining the presence of consciousness, and then discusses the strengths and weaknesses of the case for and against the hypothesis that LLMs are conscious according to each criterion. It then summarizes the overall case, concluding that the probability that LLMs are conscious is not high for current models but could be significant for models in the next decade.

## Strengths
The paper is excellently written, and does a thorough and balanced job of surveying the arguments for and against the hypothesis that LLMs are conscious. It is likely to be very helpful to many people in the field as a clear and balanced introduction to this topic.

## Weaknesses
The main weakness is that the paper is not making a novel contribution to the field. It is purely a survey of the debate, and does not advance either the cause of theist or anti-representationalist arguments. However, this is not a very significant weakness, since the paper is primarily educational rather than research.
