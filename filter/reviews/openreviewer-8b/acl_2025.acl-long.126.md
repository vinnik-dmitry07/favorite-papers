# Review

## Summary
This paper introduces INVESTOR-BENCH, a benchmark specifically designed for evaluating LLM-based agents in diverse financial decision-making contexts. The authors address two main challenges in the field: the lack of a comprehensive LLM agent framework adaptable to various financial tasks and the absence of standardized benchmarks and consistent datasets for assessing agent performance. INVESTOR-BENCH enhances the versatility of LLM-enabled agents by providing a comprehensive suite of tasks applicable to different financial products, including single equities like stocks, cryptocurrencies, and exchange-traded funds (ETFs). The authors assess the reasoning and decision-making capabilities of their agent framework using thirteen different LLMs as backbone models, across various market environments and tasks. They have also curated a diverse collection of open-source, multi-modal datasets and developed a comprehensive suite of environments for financial decision-making. This establishes a highly accessible platform for evaluating financial agents' performance across various scenarios.

## Strengths
1. The paper introduces a novel benchmark, INVESTOR-BENCH, specifically designed for evaluating LLM-based agents in diverse financial decision-making contexts. This fills a significant gap in the field, as there was previously no comprehensive framework adaptable to a variety of financial tasks.
2. The benchmark provides a comprehensive suite of tasks applicable to different financial products, including single equities like stocks, cryptocurrencies, and exchange-traded funds (ETFs). This wide range of tasks enhances the versatility of LLM-enabled agents and allows for a more thorough evaluation of their capabilities.
3. The authors assess the reasoning and decision-making capabilities of their agent framework using thirteen different LLMs as backbone models, across various market environments and tasks. This provides a robust evaluation of the benchmark's effectiveness and the performance of different LLMs in financial decision-making.

## Weaknesses
1. The paper does not provide a detailed analysis of the limitations of the proposed benchmark or potential biases in the data used. Understanding the limitations and biases is crucial for interpreting the results and for future improvements of the benchmark.
2. The paper does not provide a detailed analysis of the interpretability of the results, which is an important aspect of evaluating the decision-making capabilities of LLMs. Understanding why certain models perform better than others is crucial for practical applications.
3. The paper does not provide a detailed analysis of the computational resources required to run the experiments, including the hardware specifications and the amount of time needed for the experiments. This information is important for reproducibility and for understanding the practical feasibility of using the benchmark in real-world scenarios.

## Questions
1. What are the main limitations of the proposed benchmark, and how can they be addressed in future work?
2. How do the results of the evaluation of different LLMs vary depending on the specific financial task and market environment?
3. What are the computational resources required to run the experiments, and how do they vary depending on the size and complexity of the LLMs used?
