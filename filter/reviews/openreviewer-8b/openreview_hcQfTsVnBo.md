# Review

## Summary
This paper reverse-engineers a one-hidden layer neural network that has grokked the arithmetic of the permutation groups $S_5$ and $S_6$. The models discover the true subgroup structure of the full group and converge on neural circuits that decompose the group arithmetic using the permutation group’s subgroups. The authors compare their work to Chughtai et al. [4] which also claims to find a different algorithm for this same problem.

## Strengths
- The paper is well-written and easy to follow. The authors do a good job of explaining the mathematical concepts in a simple and clear manner.
- The paper provides a detailed analysis of the model's mechanisms and confirms that the proposed theory is a faithful description of the circuit's functionality.
- The paper compares its findings with existing literature and highlights the differences and similarities.

## Weaknesses
- The paper focuses on a specific problem of reversing engineering neural networks that learn group multiplication. While this is a good starting point, it would be interesting to see how the techniques and insights gained from this work can be applied to more complex and real-world tasks.
- The paper mentions that the model's architecture is based on Chughtai et al. [4] but it would be helpful to provide more details on the model's design and how it differs from existing architectures used for similar tasks.
- The paper does not provide any code or implementation details, which could limit the reproducibility of the results.

## Questions
- How do the techniques used in this work apply to more complex and real-world tasks beyond group multiplication?
- Can the authors provide more details on the model's architecture and how it differs from existing architectures used for similar tasks?
- Can the authors provide code or implementation details to facilitate the reproducibility of the results?
