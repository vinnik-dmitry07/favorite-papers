# Review

## Summary
This paper proposes a novel algorithm called Hierarchy-Aware Credit Assignment (HICRA) to enhance the reasoning capabilities of LLMs through reinforcement learning. The authors identify a hierarchical reasoning process in LLMs, where low-level skills are learned first, followed by a focus on high-level strategic planning. HICRA concentrates optimization efforts on high-impact planning tokens, leading to significant performance improvements. The paper also discusses related concepts like "aha moments" and "length-scaling" in the context of this hierarchical reasoning.

## Strengths
1. The concept of an emergent reasoning hierarchy in LLMs is novel and provides a new perspective on how these models learn and improve their reasoning abilities.
2. The development of HICRA, which targets high-impact planning tokens, is a significant contribution, potentially leading to more efficient training of LLMs.
3. The paper provides a thorough analysis of the hierarchical reasoning process, using various metrics and experiments to validate its claims.
4. The insights into phenomena like "aha moments" and "length-scaling" add depth to the understanding of LLM behavior during reasoning.

## Weaknesses
1. The paper could benefit from a more detailed comparison with existing methods and algorithms in the field of reinforcement learning and LLM training.
2. While the experimental results are promising, the paper could provide more information on the scalability of HICRA and its applicability to different types of reasoning tasks.
3. The paper could include more case studies or examples to illustrate the practical application of HICRA and the hierarchical reasoning process.

## Questions
1. How does HICRA compare to other state-of-the-art methods in terms of computational efficiency and resource requirements?
2. Can the authors provide more examples or case studies to illustrate the hierarchical reasoning process in LLMs and the impact of HICRA?
3. How generalizable is the emergent reasoning hierarchy concept to other types of reasoning tasks beyond mathematical problem-solving?
