# A social path to human-like artificial intelligence

## Summary
The paper proposes that the key to creating human-like artificial intelligence is to incorporate social mechanisms that have shaped human evolution. The authors argue that intelligence is not a solo endeavor but is instead a product of interactions among agents, and that many of the key mechanisms that drive innovation and adaptation in human societies can be applied to AI systems. The paper presents a compelling case for a more social, collaborative, and evolutionary approach to AI development.

## Strengths
The paper is well-written and well-structured, with clear explanations of complex concepts and a logical flow of ideas. The authors provide concrete examples of how social mechanisms can be applied to AI, drawing from a range of fields including evolutionary biology, anthropology, and AI research. The paper also presents a thorough review of existing literature and research, demonstrating a deep understanding of the relevant theories and findings in this area.

## Weaknesses
The paper could benefit from a more detailed discussion of the potential challenges and drawbacks of incorporating social mechanisms into AI development. For example, how might these mechanisms affect AI systems in different domains, and how can we ensure that the resulting AI systems are stable and aligned with human values? 

The paper could also provide more concrete examples of how the proposed mechanisms can be implemented in practical AI systems, and what the potential outcomes might be. For instance, how might Machiavellian selection or cumulative culture be operationalized in AI systems, and what kind of behaviors or innovations might emerge as a result?

Finally, the paper could address the potential impact of these social mechanisms on AI system safety and security. How might these mechanisms affect the robustness and reliability of AI systems, and what measures can be taken to mitigate any negative consequences?

## Questions
1. How do the authors envision the role of human-like intelligence in the context of AI systems? Is the goal to create AI systems that are indistinguishable from humans, or to create systems that possess human-like intelligence but with superhuman capabilities?

2. What are the potential challenges and drawbacks of incorporating social mechanisms into AI development, and how can these be mitigated?

3. How might the proposed mechanisms be implemented in practical AI systems, and what are the potential outcomes?

4. What are the potential impacts of these social mechanisms on AI system safety and security, and how can these be addressed?
