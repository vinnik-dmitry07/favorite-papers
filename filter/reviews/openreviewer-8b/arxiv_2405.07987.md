# Review

## Summary
This paper presents a hypothesis that representations learned by different models, trained on different tasks and with different architectures, are converging to a common platonic representation, which is a statistical model of the underlying reality. The authors provide evidence from various lines of work to support this hypothesis, including model stitching, alignment of representations with model scale, alignment of representations across modalities, and alignment with biological representations. They discuss various factors that may contribute to this convergence, including data scaling, model scaling, and simplicity bias. They also explore the implications of this convergence, such as the potential for training models on shared cross-modal data and reduced hallucination and bias with scale. Finally, they acknowledge various limitations and counterexamples to their analysis, such as the possibility of different modalities containing different information and not all representations being converging.

## Strengths
- The paper presents a novel and intriguing hypothesis about the convergence of representations in different AI models, which has not been extensively explored before.
- The authors provide a comprehensive overview of relevant literature and evidence from various fields to support their hypothesis, such as model stitching, alignment of representations with model scale, alignment of representations across modalities, and alignment with biological representations.
- The paper is well-structured and clearly written, with helpful figures and tables to illustrate key points.
- The authors acknowledge various limitations and counterexamples to their analysis, demonstrating a critical and nuanced perspective on the topic.

## Weaknesses
- The paper could benefit from a more detailed discussion on the implications of the platonic representation hypothesis for future research and development in AI, such as how it might influence the design of new models or training strategies.
- While the authors mention the limitations and counterexamples, they could provide more in-depth analysis and potential solutions to address these limitations, such as how to handle different modalities containing different information.
- The paper could explore more explicitly the potential trade-offs and challenges of relying on a shared representation across modalities, such as potential loss of specialized knowledge or flexibility.

## Questions
- How might the platonic representation hypothesis influence the design of new AI models or training strategies?
- How can we better handle the limitations and counterexamples mentioned, such as different modalities containing different information?
- What are the potential trade-offs and challenges of relying on a shared representation across modalities, and how can we mitigate these if necessary?
