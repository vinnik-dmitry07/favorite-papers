# Review

## Summary
This paper proposes a framework for classifying the capabilities and behavior of Artificial General Intelligence (AGI) models and their precursors. The framework introduces levels of AGI performance and generality, providing a common language to compare models, assess risks, and measure progress along the path to AGI. The authors analyze existing definitions of AGI and distill six principles that a useful ontology for AGI should satisfy. They propose “Levels of AGI” based on depth (performance) and breadth (generality) of capabilities, and reflect on how current systems fit into this ontology. The authors also discuss the challenging requirements for future benchmarks that quantify the behavior and capabilities of AGI models against these levels.

## Strengths
1. The paper provides a comprehensive analysis of existing definitions of AGI and identifies commonalities and properties that contribute to a clear, operationalizable definition of AGI.
2. The paper introduces a two-dimensional, level-based ontology for AGI that focuses on performance and generality, providing a nuanced way to define progress toward AGI.
3. The paper discusses the challenging requirements for future benchmarks that quantify the behavior and capabilities of AGI models against the proposed levels.
4. The paper emphasizes the importance of carefully selecting Human-AI Interaction paradigms for responsible and safe deployment of highly capable AI systems.

## Weaknesses
1. The paper does not propose a benchmark in this paper, but only clarifies the ontology a benchmark should attempt to measure.
2. The paper does not provide a detailed framework for implementing the proposed levels of AGI and autonomy, but only provide some principles.

## Questions
1. How can the proposed framework be used to evaluate existing AI systems and provide a more nuanced understanding of their capabilities and limitations?
2. What are the implications of the proposed framework for developing new AI systems and achieving AGI?
3. How can the proposed framework be used to inform policy and regulatory decisions related to AI development and deployment?
