# Review

## Summary
The authors present a review of the evidence for the role of language in human cognition, arguing that language is primarily a tool for communication rather than for thought. They present evidence from neuroscience, psychology, and linguistics to support their argument.

## Strengths
The paper is well written and clear. The arguments are well supported by the evidence presented. This is an important topic, and the authors make a compelling case for their position.

## Weaknesses
The authors do not adequately address the strong version of the "language of thought" hypothesis, i.e., the idea that thinking is fundamentally in language (see Smolensky, 1987; Van Gelder, 1990). Instead, they focus on a weaker version of the hypothesis, which allows for the possibility of non-linguistic mental representations (e.g., Pinker, 1991). The strong version of the hypothesis is compatible with the authors' evidence and arguments, and should be addressed directly.
