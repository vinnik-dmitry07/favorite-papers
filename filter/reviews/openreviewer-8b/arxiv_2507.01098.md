# Review

## Summary
This paper provides a detailed explanation of the proof of the perfect Platonic Representation Hypothesis (PRH) for the embedded deep linear network model (EDLN), showing that two EDLNs with different widths and depths, trained on different data, will become perfectly platonic. It also discusses how the emergence of platonic representations is related to the emergence of progressive sharpening, suggesting that these two phenomena have a common cause. The paper highlights the importance of understanding the role of "entropic forces" in representation learning.

## Strengths
The paper provides a clear and detailed explanation of the proof of the perfect Platonic Representation Hypothesis (PRH) for the embedded deep linear network model (EDLN). The paper is well-written and well-organized, making it easy to follow the authors' reasoning and arguments. The paper addresses an important topic in the field of representation learning and provides insights into the emergence of platonic representations in neural networks.

## Weaknesses
The paper focuses on a specific model (EDLN) and a specific setting, and it is unclear how well the results and insights presented in the paper generalize to other models and settings. The paper does not provide any empirical results or experiments to support the theoretical analysis and insights presented. A more detailed discussion of the limitations of the proposed approach and potential directions for future research would strengthen the paper.

## Questions
1. Can the authors provide more details on how the perfect PRH might generalize to other models and settings beyond EDLN?
2. Are there any empirical results or experiments that can support the theoretical analysis and insights presented in the paper?
3. Can the authors discuss more about the role of "entropic forces" in representation learning and how it relates to the emergence of platonic representations?
