# Review

## Summary
This paper introduces a new learning paradigm called Nested Learning (NL), which represents machine learning models as nested, multi-level optimization problems. The authors argue that current deep learning methods compress their own context flow and that in-context learning emerges in large models within this framework. They propose three core contributions: Expressive Optimizers, Self-Modifying Learning Module, and Continuum Memory System. The paper also presents a new optimizer called Delta Gradient Descent (DGD) and a continual learning module called Hope.

## Strengths
1. The paper introduces a new learning paradigm, Nested Learning (NL), which offers a fresh perspective on representing machine learning models as nested, multi-level optimization problems.
2. The authors provide a neuroscientific motivation for NL, drawing insights from the human brain's memory consolidation processes and neural oscillations.
3. The paper presents three core contributions: Expressive Optimizers, Self-Modifying Learning Module, and Continuum Memory System, which enhance the capabilities of machine learning models.

## Weaknesses
1. The paper's structure and presentation could be improved for clarity. The introduction and motivation are lengthy, while the experimental section is brief and lacks detail.
2. The experimental section is too brief and lacks sufficient detail. The evaluation of the proposed methods is limited, and the results are not thoroughly discussed.
3. The paper does not provide a detailed comparison with existing methods, making it difficult to assess the novelty and effectiveness of the proposed approach.
4. The paper lacks a detailed analysis of the computational complexity and resource requirements of the proposed methods.
5. The paper does not provide a thorough discussion of the limitations of the proposed methods and potential areas for future research.
