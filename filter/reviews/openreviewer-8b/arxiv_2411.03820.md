# Review

## Summary
This paper presents a new algorithm called "Beyond the Rainbow" (BTR) that combines six independent improvements to the Rainbow DQN algorithm, achieving state-of-the-art performance on the Atari-60 benchmark and demonstrating the ability to train agents for modern games such as Super Mario Galaxy, Mario Kart, and Mortal Kombat. The authors conduct ablation studies to analyze the impact of each component on performance and other measures, and provide a detailed analysis of the algorithm's behavior. The paper also discusses the accessibility of BTR, which can be trained on a desktop PC in under 12 hours, compared to 35 hours for Rainbow DQN, making it more accessible to smaller research labs and hobbyists.

## Strengths
1. The paper is well-written and easy to follow, with a clear explanation of the algorithm and its components.
2. The authors provide a thorough analysis of the algorithm's performance and behavior, including ablation studies and other measures.
3. The algorithm achieves state-of-the-art performance on the Atari-60 benchmark and demonstrates the ability to train agents for modern games, which is a significant accomplishment.

## Weaknesses
1. The paper does not compare BTR with other state-of-the-art algorithms such as Agent57, MEME, and Dreamer-v3, which are mentioned in the related work section. It would be beneficial to include comparisons with these algorithms in terms of performance and computational requirements.
2. While the paper claims that BTR is more accessible to smaller research labs and hobbyists, it would be helpful to provide more detailed information on the hardware requirements and training time for these modern games.
3. The paper does not provide a detailed analysis of the algorithm's limitations or potential areas for improvement. It would be beneficial to include a discussion of the algorithm's weaknesses and potential future directions for research.

## Questions
1. How does BTR compare to other state-of-the-art algorithms such as Agent57, MEME, and Dreamer-v3 in terms of performance and computational requirements?
2. What are the hardware requirements and training time for BTR to train agents for the modern games mentioned in the paper?
3. Can you provide more details on the potential areas for improvement and limitations of BTR? How might these be addressed in future research?
