# Review

## Summary
The authors argue that the phenomenon of "emergent abilities" in large language models (LLMs) is largely due to the choice of metrics rather than an actual change in model capabilities. They provide a mathematical model to explain this and test their hypothesis on the InstructGPT/GPT-3 family of models on arithmetic tasks. They also perform a meta-analysis of the BIG-Bench dataset and show that emergent abilities are mostly due to the use of non-linear or discontinuous metrics. They demonstrate how to induce emergent abilities in vision tasks by changing the metrics.

## Strengths
- The paper provides a clear and compelling argument that challenges the notion of emergent abilities in LLMs, which is a significant contribution to the field.
- The authors present a mathematical model that explains how the choice of metrics can lead to the appearance of emergent abilities, which is a novel way of looking at the problem.
- The paper includes a thorough analysis of the InstructGPT/GPT-3 models on arithmetic tasks, as well as a meta-analysis of BIG-Bench, providing strong evidence for their claims.

## Weaknesses
- The paper focuses primarily on the GPT family of models and BIG-Bench dataset, which may not be representative of other LLMs or tasks.
- The authors could provide more details on how to choose appropriate metrics for different tasks and model sizes to avoid the appearance of emergent abilities.

## Questions
- How do the authors suggest researchers choose appropriate metrics for their tasks to avoid the appearance of emergent abilities?
- Do the authors have any suggestions for how to design experiments to test for emergent abilities in a more controlled way?
- How do the authors think the choice of metrics relates to the overall performance of the model, not just emergent abilities?
