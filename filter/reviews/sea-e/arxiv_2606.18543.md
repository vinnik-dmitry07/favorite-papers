 **Summary:**
The paper introduces CEO-Bench, a novel benchmark designed to evaluate the long-term strategic capabilities of language models in managing a startup over a 500-day period. The benchmark simulates a startup environment where agents can make decisions related to pricing, marketing, budgeting, and other aspects of business management. The paper evaluates various language models and compares their performance against a rule-based baseline, highlighting the challenges of long-term planning, information gathering, and adaptability in dynamic environments. The benchmark is designed to be challenging, with stochasticity and non-stationary environments, and it provides a detailed analysis of the performance of different models. However, the paper is criticized for its limited scope, lack of diversity in model evaluations, and insufficient discussion on the limitations and potential biases in the benchmark design.

**Strengths:**
- The paper introduces a novel and challenging benchmark, CEO-Bench, which evaluates the long-term strategic capabilities of language models in managing a startup over a 500-day period.
- The benchmark is designed to be realistic and challenging, with stochasticity and non-stationary environments, and it provides a detailed analysis of the performance of different models.
- The paper is well-written, with clear descriptions of the benchmark and the experiments conducted, and it includes a detailed description of the CEO-Bench simulator and the design principles that make the task challenging and realistic.
- The paper includes a good discussion of the limitations of the benchmark, which is appreciated for its transparency.
- The paper provides a comprehensive evaluation of various language models, including both open-source and proprietary models, which helps in understanding the current capabilities and limitations of these models.

**Weaknesses:**
- The paper lacks a detailed discussion on the limitations and potential biases in the benchmark design, which could affect the validity and reliability of the results.
- The scope of the benchmark is limited, focusing only on managing a startup, which may not adequately represent the full range of strategic capabilities required in real-world scenarios.
- The paper does not include a diverse set of models for evaluation, which limits the generalizability of the findings.
- The paper lacks a detailed discussion on the methodology used to evaluate the models, which could affect the reproducibility and transparency of the results.
- The paper does not provide sufficient details on the implementation of the rule-based baseline, which could affect the comparability and validity of the results.
- The paper does not discuss the potential societal impacts of the benchmark, which is a significant omission in the ethical considerations section.

**Questions:**
- Could the authors provide more details on the methodology used to evaluate the models, including the specific metrics and methods used to assess the strategic capabilities of the models?
- How do the authors address the potential biases and limitations in the benchmark design, and what steps have been taken to ensure the validity and reliability of the results?
- Could the authors provide more details on the implementation of the rule-based baseline, including the specific actions and strategies used to manage the startup?
- How do the authors plan to expand the scope of the benchmark to include a broader range of strategic capabilities, and what steps are being taken to ensure the benchmark remains relevant and challenging as technology advances?
- Could the authors discuss the potential societal impacts of the benchmark, including the potential for misuse or unintended consequences, and how these risks are being mitigated?
