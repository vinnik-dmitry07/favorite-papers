 **Summary:**
The paper discusses the role of language in human cognition, challenging the notion that language is a primary tool for thought. It presents evidence from neuroscience, psychology, and anthropology to argue that language is primarily a tool for communication, not a means of internal thought. The authors propose that language and thought are distinct processes, with evidence suggesting that language is a cultural tool that evolved to facilitate communication and the transmission of knowledge. The paper also explores the neural underpinnings of language and its relationship to other cognitive processes, suggesting that language and thought are interconnected but distinct. The paper is well-received for its clear writing, comprehensive review of evidence, and thought-provoking arguments, although some reviewers express concerns about the paper's conclusions and the strength of the evidence presented.

**Strengths:**
- The paper is well-written, clear, and accessible, making complex ideas accessible to a broad audience.
- It presents a comprehensive review of evidence from various fields, including neuroscience, psychology, and anthropology, to support its arguments.
- The paper challenges the dominant view that language is a primary tool for thought, offering a fresh perspective on the role of language in human cognition.
- The authors effectively argue that language is a tool for communication, not a means of internal thought, and that this perspective has implications for how we understand the relationship between language and thought.
- The paper is significant in its potential to influence the field of cognitive science and the study of language, offering a new perspective on the role of language in human cognition.

**Weaknesses:**
- The paper's conclusions are not convincingly supported by the evidence presented, and some of the evidence is not directly relevant to the main argument.
- The paper's claims about the role of language in thought are not well-supported by the evidence, and the evidence presented does not convincingly demonstrate that language is not a tool for thought.
- The paper's writing style is sometimes overly simplistic and lacks depth in some areas, which could make it difficult for readers to fully grasp the complex ideas presented.
- There is a lack of clarity in the paper's arguments, particularly in the relationship between language and thought, which could make it difficult for readers to understand the implications of the authors' claims.
- The paper does not adequately address the role of language in shaping thought, which is a significant limitation in the discussion of the relationship between language and cognition.

**Questions:**
- Can the authors clarify how the evidence presented supports the main argument that language is a tool for communication rather than a means of internal thought?
- How does the paper address the role of language in shaping thought, particularly in light of the evidence suggesting that language can influence cognitive processes?
- Could the authors expand on the evidence presented to more convincingly support their claims about the role of language in communication versus thought?
- How does the paper's argument about the role of language in communication differ from the view that language is a tool for thought, particularly in terms of the cognitive processes involved?
- Could the authors provide more detailed examples or evidence to support their claims about the neural underpinnings of language and its relationship to other cognitive processes?
