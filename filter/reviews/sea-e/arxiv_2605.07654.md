 **Summary:**
The paper introduces a novel method for improving the reliability of large language models (LLMs) in generating correct answers to reasoning tasks. The method, termed prefix consistency, involves truncating a sample's chain-of-thought (CoT) and regenerating the remainder to check for consistency. This consistency is used as a reliability signal to weight candidate answers, which are then aggregated using a weighted majority vote. The method is evaluated across various benchmarks and models, showing improvements over existing methods in terms of accuracy and cost-efficiency. The authors demonstrate that prefix consistency can lead to significant reductions in the number of tokens required to reach a certain accuracy threshold, making it a promising approach for enhancing the reliability of LLMs in generating answers.

**Strengths:**
- The paper is well-written, clear, and easy to follow, with a clear motivation and a well-defined problem statement.
- The proposed method is simple, intuitive, and effective, with a straightforward and easy-to-implement approach.
- The method is evaluated on a wide range of models and benchmarks, demonstrating its effectiveness across various scenarios.
- The paper provides a thorough empirical analysis, including a detailed comparison with existing methods and a comprehensive evaluation of the method's performance.
- The method is shown to be cost-efficient, requiring fewer tokens to reach a certain accuracy threshold compared to standard majority voting.
- The paper presents a novel approach to reliability signals that does not require access to token log-probabilities or self-rating prompts, which is a significant advantage in terms of computational efficiency.

**Weaknesses:**
- The paper lacks a detailed discussion on the limitations of the proposed method, particularly in scenarios where the method might not perform well or where it might introduce biases.
- The paper does not include a discussion on the potential negative societal impacts of the proposed method.
- There is a lack of comparison with some important baselines, such as P(True) and DeepConf, which could have provided a more robust evaluation of the method's effectiveness.
- The paper does not provide a detailed analysis of the computational cost of the proposed method compared to other methods, which could be crucial for practical implementation.
- The paper does not discuss the potential impact of the method on the diversity of answers generated by LLMs, which could be a significant concern in some applications.
- The paper does not include a discussion on how the method might perform in scenarios where the correct answer is not the mode of the answer distribution, which is a common scenario in LLM-based reasoning tasks.

**Questions:**
- Could the authors provide more details on how the method performs in scenarios where the correct answer is not the mode of the answer distribution?
- How does the method handle the potential for biases in the regenerated answers, especially in scenarios where the LLM might have biases in its reasoning?
- Could the authors discuss the potential impact of the method on the diversity of answers generated by LLMs?
- How does the method compare in terms of computational cost to other methods, particularly in terms of the number of tokens required for regeneration?
- Could the authors provide more details on the computational cost of the proposed method compared to other methods, including the time required for regeneration and the overall computational overhead?
- How does the method perform in scenarios where the LLM is not well-calibrated, and what are the implications for the reliability of the generated answers?
