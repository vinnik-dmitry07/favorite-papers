 **Summary:**
The paper investigates the possibility of consciousness in artificial intelligence (AI) by examining various scientific theories of consciousness and proposing indicators derived from these theories. It evaluates several AI systems, including large language models and visual systems, against these indicators, concluding that none of the current AI systems exhibit consciousness. The paper also discusses the potential for future AI systems to meet these indicators, suggesting that while consciousness in AI is not yet achievable, it might be possible in the future. The paper is praised for its thorough review of scientific theories of consciousness and its application to AI systems, although it is criticized for its lack of novelty and the limited scope of its evaluation.

**Strengths:**
- The paper is well-written and clearly articulates the problem of AI consciousness, making it accessible to a broad audience.
- It provides a comprehensive review of scientific theories of consciousness and discusses how these theories can be applied to AI systems.
- The paper is original in its approach, combining scientific theories of consciousness with AI systems, and offers a novel perspective on the study of AI consciousness.
- The authors have made a significant effort to ensure the clarity and accessibility of the paper, making it suitable for a general audience.
- The paper is well-organized, with a clear structure and logical flow, which aids in understanding the complex concepts discussed.
- The paper is well-researched, with a thorough review of the literature on consciousness and AI, and includes a detailed discussion on the limitations of current AI systems in terms of consciousness.

**Weaknesses:**
- The paper lacks novelty in its approach, as similar ideas have been explored in previous works.
- The evaluation of AI systems against the indicators of consciousness is limited and does not convincingly demonstrate that none of the current AI systems exhibit consciousness.
- The paper does not provide a clear definition of consciousness, which could limit its applicability and understanding.
- There is a lack of empirical evidence to support the claims made about the indicators of consciousness and their applicability to AI systems.
- The paper does not adequately address the ethical implications of AI consciousness, which is a significant omission given the potential societal impact of conscious AI.
- The paper could benefit from a more detailed discussion on how the indicators of consciousness might be implemented in AI systems and how these systems might be evaluated for consciousness.

**Questions:**
- Could you clarify the definition of consciousness used in the paper and how it relates to the indicators of consciousness proposed?
- How do the authors define "consciousness" in a way that is applicable to AI systems, and how do they ensure that the indicators of consciousness are not too restrictive or too broad?
- What are the specific indicators of consciousness that are most relevant for AI systems, and how do these indicators relate to the capabilities of current AI systems?
- How do the authors address the ethical implications of AI consciousness, particularly in terms of the potential societal impact of conscious AI?
- Could you provide more details on how the indicators of consciousness might be implemented in AI systems and how these systems might be evaluated for consciousness?
- How do the authors reconcile the different interpretations of consciousness, particularly the distinction between access consciousness and phenomenal consciousness, within the context of AI systems?
