 **Summary:**
The paper investigates the effectiveness of Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) in enhancing the reasoning capabilities of large language models (LLMs). It critically examines the performance of RLVR-trained models against their base models across various benchmarks, including mathematical reasoning, code generation, and visual reasoning. The study reveals that RLVR models, while improving performance in certain scenarios, do not consistently surpass the reasoning capabilities of their base models. The authors propose that this might be due to the pre-trained model's bias, which limits the exploration of new reasoning patterns. The paper also discusses potential improvements in RLVR training, such as using distillation or different RL algorithms, but concludes that these do not significantly enhance the models' reasoning capabilities beyond those of the base models.

**Strengths:**
- The paper provides a comprehensive analysis of the current state of RLVR training, including detailed experiments and ablation studies that cover a variety of RL algorithms and models.
- The authors have conducted extensive experiments and ablation studies to understand the effects of different RL algorithms and training configurations on the performance of RLVR models.
- The paper is well-written, clear, and easy to follow, with a thorough discussion of the limitations of RLVR training and potential directions for future research.
- The findings of the paper are significant and contribute to the ongoing discussion about the effectiveness of RLVR training in enhancing the reasoning capabilities of LLMs.
- The paper is well-organized and includes a detailed analysis of the reasoning paths generated by RLVR models, which are shown to be similar to those of the base models.

**Weaknesses:**
- The paper lacks a clear explanation of the experimental setup and the specifics of the RLVR training process, which could make it difficult for readers to replicate or understand the results fully.
- There is a lack of discussion on the impact of different RL algorithms on the performance of RLVR models, which could be crucial for understanding the effectiveness of different training methods.
- The paper does not adequately address the potential biases in the RLVR training process, such as the influence of the base model's prior on the reasoning capabilities of the RLVR models.
- The paper could benefit from a more detailed discussion on the generalizability of the findings to other types of tasks or models, as well as a more thorough analysis of the impact of different training parameters on the performance of RLVR models.
- The paper does not sufficiently explore the potential of RLVR training in enhancing the reasoning capabilities of LLMs beyond those of the base models, which is a significant limitation given the current state of the technology.
