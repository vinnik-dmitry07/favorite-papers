 **Summary:**
The paper explores the concept of open-ended learning (OEL) in artificial agents, focusing on how they generate novelty and adapt to new environments. It critiques existing definitions of OEL and proposes a new framework that distinguishes between open-ended processes and open-ended learning problems. The authors argue that open-ended learning is characterized by the generation of novelty from an observer's perspective, and propose a formal definition to differentiate it from other related concepts like continual learning. The paper also discusses the challenges in evaluating and comparing open-ended learning agents, suggesting potential metrics and methodologies. Despite its theoretical contributions, the paper lacks empirical validation and a clear, concise definition, which limits its practical applicability and clarity.

**Strengths:**
- The paper provides a comprehensive review of the literature on open-ended learning (OEL), tracing its history and various definitions from different perspectives.
- The authors propose a formal definition of open-ended learning, which is a significant contribution to the field.
- The paper is well-written, clear, and easy to follow, making it accessible to a broad audience.
- It introduces a novel concept of open-ended learning problems, which is a valuable contribution to the field.
- The discussion on the evaluation of open-ended learning agents is insightful and provides a practical approach to assessing the effectiveness of OEL systems.

**Weaknesses:**
- The paper lacks a clear, concise definition of open-ended learning, which could make it more accessible and understandable to a broader audience.
- There is a lack of empirical validation for the proposed definition and framework, which limits the paper's practical applicability.
- The paper does not provide a clear distinction between open-ended learning and other related concepts like continual learning, which could lead to confusion.
- The paper could benefit from a more detailed discussion on the evaluation of open-ended learning agents and the practical implications of the proposed framework.
- The paper's structure and organization could be improved to better guide the reader through the complex concepts and definitions presented.

**Questions:**
- Could the authors clarify the distinction between open-ended learning and continual learning, particularly in terms of the generation of novelty and the role of the environment in providing new tasks?
- How do the authors envision the practical implementation of the proposed framework, especially in terms of evaluating the effectiveness of open-ended learning agents?
- Could the authors provide examples or case studies that illustrate the application of the proposed framework in real-world scenarios?
- How do the authors address the issue of evaluating the performance of open-ended learning agents, especially in the absence of a clear performance metric?
- Could the paper benefit from a more detailed discussion on the evaluation of open-ended learning agents, possibly including a section dedicated to this topic?
