 **Summary:**
The paper introduces M3M, a novel multilingual knowledge graph question answering (KGQA) system designed to handle one-hop questions in multiple languages. M3M leverages a multilingual BERT model for question encoding and pre-trained graph embeddings to represent KG nodes and edges. It utilizes a text-to-graph projection method to map question text embeddings to the subject, predicate, and object of the KG triple corresponding to the question-answer pair. The system is evaluated on various datasets and shown to outperform existing methods such as T5 QA systems and other embedding-based methods. The paper also provides an open-source implementation, which includes pre-trained models and an API, to facilitate integration into other applications.

**Strengths:**
- The paper introduces a novel multilingual approach to one-hop knowledge graph question answering (KGQA), which is a significant contribution to the field.
- The methodology is well-explained, with clear descriptions of the proposed method and its implementation, including the use of multilingual BERT for question encoding and pre-trained graph embeddings.
- The paper provides a comprehensive evaluation of the proposed method, comparing it with strong baselines and demonstrating its effectiveness through various datasets and languages.
- The availability of open-source code and pre-trained models is a valuable resource for the community, enhancing the reproducibility and applicability of the research.
- The paper is well-written, making it easy to follow, and the experiments are extensive, covering a range of datasets and languages.

**Weaknesses:**
- The paper lacks a detailed discussion on the limitations of the proposed method, which could provide a more balanced view of its capabilities and potential challenges.
- The evaluation primarily focuses on one-hop questions, which may not fully demonstrate the system's performance on more complex, multi-hop questions.
- There is a need for more comprehensive comparisons with other state-of-the-art KGQA systems, including those that use large language models (LLMs) and other advanced techniques.
- The paper could benefit from a more detailed analysis of the results, particularly in terms of the impact of different components and settings on the performance.
- The paper's claims about the system's performance and the effectiveness of the proposed method are sometimes overstated, which could mislead readers about the true capabilities of the system.

**Questions:**
- Can the proposed method be extended to handle multi-hop questions, and if so, how would this be implemented?
- How does the system handle questions with multiple correct answers, and what measures are in place to ensure that the system's responses are accurate and reliable?
- Could the authors provide more details on the training and evaluation of the NER model used in the system?
- How does the system handle questions that are not in the training data, and what strategies are used to ensure that the system can generalize to new, unseen questions?
- Is there a possibility to include more complex datasets in future evaluations to test the system's performance on more challenging question types?
