 **Summary:**
The paper introduces RIFT, a taxonomy for evaluating the quality of rubrics used in large language model (LLM) benchmarks and training pipelines. RIFT identifies eight failure modes categorized into three groups: reliability, content validity, and consequential validity. The taxonomy was developed through an iterative process involving human annotators and grounded theory, and its effectiveness was validated using inter-rater reliability and human annotation. The paper also proposes automated metrics to diagnose rubric quality, which were evaluated against human annotations, showing moderate to good alignment. Despite its strengths in methodology and the development of a novel taxonomy, the paper has been criticized for its limited scope, potential for bias, and the need for more comprehensive evaluation and clarity in presentation.

**Strengths:**
- The paper introduces a novel taxonomy for evaluating rubrics, which is a significant contribution to the field.
- The methodology used to develop the taxonomy is well-described and appears sound, with a focus on grounded theory and human annotation.
- The paper is well-written, making it easy to follow and understand, with clear explanations of the methodology and results.
- The authors provide a detailed analysis of the inter-rater reliability and the alignment of automated metrics with human annotations, which adds depth to the evaluation of the proposed taxonomy.
- The paper addresses an important and timely issue of evaluating the quality of rubrics in LLM benchmarks and training pipelines, which is crucial for ensuring the reliability and validity of LLM evaluations.

**Weaknesses:**
- The paper lacks a clear discussion of the limitations of the proposed taxonomy, which could include the potential for bias in the human annotation process, the generalizability of the taxonomy across different domains, and the scalability of the annotation process.
- The scope of the study is limited, focusing only on a small number of rubrics and not sufficiently addressing the broader applicability of the findings.
- The paper does not sufficiently address the potential for bias in the human annotation process, which could affect the validity of the results.
- The paper could benefit from more comprehensive evaluation, including a broader range of rubrics and a more rigorous comparison with existing methods.
- The presentation of the paper could be improved, particularly in terms of clarity and organization, especially in the introduction and the presentation of results.
- The paper does not sufficiently discuss the potential for bias in the human annotation process, which could affect the validity of the results.

**Questions:**
- Could the authors provide more details on the human annotation process, including the number of annotators, the criteria for selecting annotators, and the training and calibration process?
- How were the rubrics selected for the study, and were they representative of the broader population of rubrics used in LLM benchmarks and training pipelines?
- Could the authors provide more details on the inter-rater reliability analysis, including the number of annotators, the criteria for selecting annotators, and the training and calibration process?
- How do the authors plan to address the potential for bias in the human annotation process, and what steps will be taken to ensure the validity of the results?
- Could the authors clarify the presentation of the results, particularly in the introduction and the presentation of the taxonomy and failure modes?
- How do the authors plan to address the generalizability of the taxonomy across different domains, and what steps will be taken to ensure that the taxonomy remains relevant and applicable in diverse contexts?
