 **Summary:**
The paper explores the evolution of large language models (LLMs) from weight-centric to context-centric and infrastructure-centric approaches, focusing on the externalization of capabilities through memory, skills, and protocols. It argues that these externalizations are crucial for managing the complexity of LLMs, particularly in long-horizon tasks, by shifting the burden from the model's internal computation to external structures. The paper provides a comprehensive review of the historical development of these externalizations and discusses their implications for the future of LLM research and development. It also highlights the importance of harness engineering, which integrates these externalizations into a coherent runtime environment, and discusses the challenges and opportunities in this area.

**Strengths:**
- The paper provides a comprehensive review of the historical development of externalization in large language models (LLMs), from weights to context to harness, and discusses the implications of this evolution for the future of LLM research and development.
- The authors effectively argue that externalization is a critical aspect of LLM research, particularly in the context of long-horizon tasks, and that it is not just a matter of engineering convenience but a fundamental shift in how we approach the design of LLMs.
- The paper is well-written, clear, and easy to follow, with a clear structure that makes it accessible to readers. It also provides a theoretical anchor for understanding the externalization of capabilities in LLMs, which is a significant contribution to the field.
- The authors address a timely and important topic in the field of LLMs, and their discussion on the challenges and opportunities in the area of harness engineering is particularly insightful.

**Weaknesses:**
- The paper could benefit from a more detailed discussion on the limitations and challenges of externalization, particularly in terms of its impact on the interpretability and generalizability of LLMs.
- There is a lack of empirical evidence to support some of the claims made about the effectiveness of externalization in improving the reliability and generalizability of LLMs.
- The paper could be more concise in some sections, particularly in the detailed descriptions of existing works, which could be better suited for an appendix.
- The paper does not sufficiently discuss the potential negative societal impacts of externalization, such as the potential for bias amplification or the loss of interpretability.
- The paper could benefit from a more thorough discussion on the trade-offs between internal and external capabilities in LLMs, particularly in terms of the costs and benefits of each approach.

**Questions:**

- How do you see the trade-offs between internal and external capabilities in LLMs evolving in the future? Will we see a shift towards more externalized capabilities, or will there be a resurgence of interest in improving the internal capabilities of LLMs?

- How do you address the limitations and challenges of externalization, particularly in terms of its impact on the interpretability and generalizability of LLMs?
