 **Summary:**
The paper introduces an automated executor designed to implement ideas generated by large language models (LLMs) and evaluates their effectiveness through large-scale parallel GPU experiments. The system is tested on two research environments: LLM pre-training and post-training, using evolutionary search and reinforcement learning to learn from execution feedback. The experiments demonstrate that the automated executor can implement a significant fraction of ideas sampled from LLMs, with evolutionary search being particularly effective in improving performance. The paper also discusses the limitations of the current approach, including the potential for mode collapse in reinforcement learning and the need for more diverse and complex ideas in LLMs.

**Strengths:**
- The paper introduces a novel approach to automated AI research by integrating LLMs with execution feedback, which is a significant advancement in the field.
- The experiments are well-designed, with clear and insightful results that demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed methods.
- The paper is well-written, making it easy to follow and understand, and the authors have provided a detailed description of the experimental setup, including the code execution LLM and the automated executor.
- The authors have conducted a thorough analysis of the training dynamics and the generated ideas, which is crucial for understanding the performance of the models.
- The paper is innovative in its approach, using LLMs to generate ideas and then implementing and evaluating them, which is a promising direction for future research.

**Weaknesses:**
- The paper lacks a discussion on the diversity of ideas generated by LLMs, which is crucial for ensuring the novelty and effectiveness of the generated ideas.
- There is a need for more detailed analysis on the limitations of the current approach, particularly in terms of the scalability and generalizability of the results.
- The paper does not adequately address the issue of mode collapse in reinforcement learning, which could limit the practical applicability of the findings.
- The paper could benefit from a more detailed discussion on the limitations of the current approach and the potential for future research directions.
- The paper does not sufficiently compare its findings with existing methods, which could help in understanding the novelty and effectiveness of the proposed approach.
- The paper could benefit from a more comprehensive discussion on the ethical implications of using LLMs for research, particularly in terms of the reliability and trustworthiness of the generated ideas.
