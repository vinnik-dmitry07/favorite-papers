 **Summary:**
The paper explores the relationship between causal models and robust adaptation in machine learning agents, specifically focusing on the necessity of causal models for generalization across distributional shifts. It argues that any agent capable of adapting to a large set of distributional shifts must have learned a causal model of the data generating process. This is supported by theoretical results that demonstrate the sufficiency and necessity of causal models for learning regret-bounded policies under domain shifts. The paper also discusses the implications of these findings for transfer learning and causal inference.
