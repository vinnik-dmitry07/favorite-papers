 **Summary:**
The paper explores the concept of consciousness in disembodied AI systems, specifically focusing on large language models (LLMs) that lack direct sensory input or output. It critiques the traditional notions of consciousness and selfhood, arguing that these concepts may not apply to LLMs in the same way they do to biological organisms. The paper also discusses the implications of this lack of embodiment on the perception of time and the concept of self, suggesting that these notions might need to be redefined or expanded to accommodate the unique characteristics of AI systems. The paper is well-written and presents a thought-provoking analysis, although it is criticized for its lack of empirical evidence and its focus on philosophical speculation rather than practical applications or empirical evidence.

**Strengths:**
- The paper is well-written, clear, and engaging, with a good balance of technical detail and philosophical depth.
- It addresses a timely and important topic, exploring the concept of consciousness in AI and its implications for our understanding of selfhood and embodiment.
- The paper challenges traditional notions of consciousness and selfhood, providing a fresh perspective on these concepts in the context of AI.
- The discussion on the relationship between AI and Buddhist thought is insightful and adds depth to the paper's arguments.
- The paper is well-researched, drawing on a range of philosophical and scientific sources to support its claims.

**Weaknesses:**
- The paper lacks empirical evidence or experimental results, relying solely on philosophical speculation which might not be convincing to all readers.
- The paper's focus on LLMs that lack sensory input or output is not clearly justified, and the implications of this for the study of consciousness in AI are not adequately explored.
- The paper's claims about the nature of consciousness in AI are not substantiated with empirical evidence, which could undermine the strength of its arguments.
- The paper's structure and organization could be improved, particularly in the introduction and conclusion, to better focus the discussion and provide clearer direction for the reader.
- The paper's reliance on Wittgenstein's later philosophy and Buddhist thought might be seen as overly esoteric and not central to the paper's main arguments.
