 **Summary:**
The paper introduces INVESTOR BENCH, a benchmark designed to evaluate the performance of large language models (LLMs) in financial decision-making tasks, including stock, cryptocurrency, and ETF trading. The benchmark integrates a comprehensive LLM-based agent framework, which includes a memory module for storing and retrieving information, and a decision-making module that processes this data to make investment recommendations. The framework is tested across various financial scenarios, using a set of open-source, multi-modal datasets and a diverse suite of environments for financial decision-making. The paper also provides a detailed evaluation of 13 LLMs, comparing their performance in different financial tasks.

**Strengths:**
- The paper introduces a novel benchmark, INVESTOR BENCH, which evaluates the reasoning and sequential decision-making capabilities of LLM-based agents in complex, open-ended financial scenarios.
- The benchmark provides a realistic perspective for assessing the design and performance of LLM-based agents, and includes open-source, multi-source market environments that closely mirror real-world conditions.
- The paper presents a unified, flexible language-agent framework that allows finance professionals to customize assessments of any LLMs serving as the agents' reasoning core.
- The framework is evaluated on three different financial decision-making tasks, and the performance of 13 LLMs is presented, providing a comprehensive evaluation of reasoning capabilities in sequential decision-making tasks within financial contexts.
- The paper is well-written, making it easy to understand, and the authors provide a detailed description of the framework and the benchmark, which is beneficial for reproducibility.

**Weaknesses:**
- The paper lacks a detailed description of the memory module, which is a crucial component of the framework. More details on how the memory is structured, how it is updated, and how it contributes to the decision-making process would be beneficial.
- The evaluation of the framework is limited to three financial decision-making tasks, which might not be sufficient to fully demonstrate the framework's capabilities.
- The paper does not provide a detailed discussion on the limitations of the framework, which could help in understanding the scope and applicability of the results.
