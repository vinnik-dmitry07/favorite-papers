 **Summary:**
The paper introduces a new benchmark, SUP-NATINST, which consists of 1616 diverse NLP tasks, each with associated task instructions. This benchmark is used to train a model, TK-INSTRUCT, which demonstrates strong performance in generalizing to unseen tasks. The model is evaluated against various baselines and shows significant improvements over existing models like InstructGPT. The paper also explores the impact of model size, training data, and task diversity on the model's performance. Despite its strengths, the paper has been criticized for its limited novelty and potential overclaiming of its contributions.

**Strengths:**
- The paper introduces a new benchmark, SUP-NATINST, which is a significant contribution to the field of NLP, providing a comprehensive dataset of 1616 tasks and their associated instructions.
- The paper is well-written, making it easy to follow and understand, and the experiments are well-designed, providing a clear and comprehensive evaluation of the proposed method.
- The authors have conducted extensive experiments, including ablation studies and analyses of the impact of model size, training data, and task diversity on the model's performance.
- The paper includes a detailed analysis of the impact of different instructional elements and input encodings on the model's performance, which is valuable for understanding the effectiveness of different instructional strategies.
- The paper is well-positioned within the literature, providing a clear and concise summary of related work and effectively demonstrating the novelty and significance of the proposed method.

**Weaknesses:**
- The paper's contribution is somewhat limited, as it primarily involves fine-tuning a pre-trained model on a large dataset and evaluating its performance on a new benchmark.
- The novelty of the work is questioned, as the methodology and dataset are seen as extensions of existing works, and the paper does not sufficiently differentiate its contributions from these prior studies.
- The paper lacks a detailed discussion on the limitations of the proposed method, which could provide a more balanced view of the research.
- There are concerns about the generalizability of the findings due to the limited number of tasks evaluated and the potential for overfitting to the specific tasks in the benchmark.
- The paper does not adequately address the potential for data leakage from the training set to the test set, which could affect the validity of the results.
- The paper could benefit from a more detailed discussion on the potential societal impacts of the research, particularly in terms of the ethical implications of the models' performance on unseen tasks.

**Questions:**
- Could the authors clarify the specific contributions of their work beyond the existing benchmarks and models, particularly in terms of the methodology and dataset design?
- How does the proposed method compare to other recent works in the field, such as those mentioned in the review?
- Can the authors provide more details on the process of collecting and curating the data for the benchmark, including the steps taken to prevent data leakage?
- How does the model perform on tasks that are not included in the training set, and what steps were taken to ensure the generalizability of the findings?
- Can the authors discuss the potential societal impacts of their research, particularly in terms of the ethical implications of the models' performance on unseen tasks?
