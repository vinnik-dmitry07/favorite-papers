 **Summary:**
The paper introduces a novel decoding strategy, self-consistency, aimed at enhancing the performance of chain-of-thought prompting in large language models (LLMs) by sampling diverse reasoning paths and selecting the most consistent answer. This approach leverages the intuition that complex reasoning problems typically admit multiple valid reasoning paths, which are aggregated to improve the accuracy and robustness of the model's responses. The method is evaluated across various benchmarks, demonstrating significant improvements over traditional greedy decoding and other baseline methods. Despite its simplicity and effectiveness, the paper acknowledges the computational cost and potential limitations in handling complex, non-natural language reasoning paths.

**Strengths:**
- The paper is well-written, clear, and easy to follow, with a clear motivation and a novel approach to improving reasoning performance in large language models (LLMs) through self-consistency.
- The proposed method is simple, effective, and requires no additional training or fine-tuning, making it accessible and practical for various applications.
- The experiments are extensive, covering a wide range of tasks and models, demonstrating the method's effectiveness and robustness across different scenarios.
- The paper provides a thorough analysis of the method's impact on different model sizes and sampling strategies, showing that it can be applied to both natural language and symbolic reasoning tasks.
- The method's simplicity and effectiveness make it a valuable contribution to the field, as it can be easily integrated into existing systems without significant modifications.

**Weaknesses:**
- The paper lacks a detailed discussion on the limitations of the self-consistency method, particularly in handling complex, non-natural language reasoning paths and the computational cost associated with sampling multiple reasoning paths.
- The method's effectiveness is not thoroughly compared with other baseline methods, such as beam search and ensemble-based approaches, which could provide a more comprehensive understanding of its relative performance.
- The paper does not address the potential for the method to introduce additional biases or errors due to the aggregation of diverse reasoning paths.
- The method's application to open-ended generation tasks and its impact on the diversity of reasoning paths generated by the model are not explored.
- The paper could benefit from a more detailed discussion on how the method performs with different model sizes and sampling strategies, as well as a more thorough analysis of the consistency of the reasoning paths generated by the model.

**Questions:**
- Can the authors provide more details on how the self-consistency method performs with different model sizes and sampling strategies, particularly in terms of the consistency of the reasoning paths and the computational cost?
- How does the self-consistency method compare with other baseline methods, such as beam search and ensemble-based approaches, in terms of performance and computational efficiency?
- Could the authors elaborate on the potential biases or errors introduced by the aggregation of diverse reasoning paths, and discuss how these might affect the overall performance and reliability of the model?
- How does the self-consistency method perform in open-ended generation tasks, and what impact does it have on the diversity of reasoning paths generated by the model?
- Could the authors provide more examples or case studies to illustrate the method's effectiveness in handling complex, non-natural language reasoning paths, and discuss any potential limitations or challenges in applying the method to such scenarios?
