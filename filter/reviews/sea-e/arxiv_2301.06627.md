 **Summary:**
The paper explores the distinction between formal and functional linguistic competence in Large Language Models (LLMs), proposing a framework that evaluates the capabilities of LLMs from a cognitive science perspective. It argues that while LLMs excel in formal linguistic competence, such as grammar and syntax, they fall short in functional linguistic competence, which involves using language in real-world situations. The authors propose that this gap arises from the training objectives of LLMs, which primarily focus on predicting the next word in a sentence, rather than on functional linguistic competence. The paper also discusses the implications of this distinction for the development of more human-like LLMs and suggests possible architectural and training modifications to address these shortcomings.
