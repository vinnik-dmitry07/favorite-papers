 **Summary:**
The paper introduces PRIMERA, a novel pre-trained model for multi-document summarization, leveraging a new pre-training objective called Entity Pyramid, which selects salient sentences based on entity frequency across documents. The model is designed to reduce the need for dataset-specific architectures and large amounts of labeled data, and is evaluated on six datasets from three different domains. The experiments demonstrate that PRIMERA outperforms current state-of-the-art models in both zero-shot and few-shot settings. The model's effectiveness is attributed to its ability to identify and aggregate salient information across multiple documents, which is crucial for multi-document summarization. The paper also includes ablation studies to validate the contributions of different components of the model.

**Strengths:**
- The paper introduces a novel pre-training objective called Entity Pyramid, which is a significant contribution to the field of multi-document summarization.
- The methodology is straightforward and easy to understand, with a clear and well-written presentation that makes the paper accessible to readers.
- The paper demonstrates the effectiveness of the proposed method through extensive experiments, showing that PRIMERA outperforms current state-of-the-art models in both zero-shot and few-shot settings.
- The paper includes ablation studies that validate the contributions of different components of the model, providing a comprehensive evaluation of the proposed method.
- The paper is well-organized, with clear explanations of the method and results, and includes detailed descriptions of the datasets used, which aids in reproducibility.

**Weaknesses:**
- The paper lacks a detailed discussion on the limitations of the proposed method, which is crucial for understanding the scope and applicability of the findings.
- The paper could benefit from more detailed explanations of the pre-training data and the specifics of the pre-training process, including the number of documents and the length of each document.
- There is a lack of clarity on the definition of "multi-document" and "single-document" in the context of the study, which could lead to confusion about the applicability of the findings.
- The paper could benefit from a more comprehensive discussion on the limitations of the proposed method, including potential negative societal impacts and ethical considerations.
- The paper does not include a user study to evaluate the quality of the generated summaries, which could provide valuable insights into the practical utility of the proposed method.

**Questions:**
- Can the authors clarify the definition of "multi-document" and "single-document" in the context of the study?
- How does the proposed method compare to other task-specific pre-training methods like PEGASUS, especially in terms of performance and efficiency?
- Could the authors provide more details on the pre-training data and the pre-training process, including the number of documents and the length of each document?
- How does the model handle the selection of salient sentences, and what criteria are used to determine which sentences are considered "salient"?
- Could the authors elaborate on the potential negative societal impacts and ethical considerations of the proposed method?
- How does the model perform in scenarios where the input documents are not well-structured or contain irrelevant or redundant information?
