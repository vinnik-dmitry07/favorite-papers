This paper introduces Dynamic Fine-Tuning (DFT), a novel method designed to enhance the generalization capabilities of Large Language Models (LLMs) when fine-tuning on tasks where only positive examples are available. The core idea behind DFT stems from a theoretical analysis that frames the standard Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) objective as a form of policy gradient reinforcement learning (RL). However, the authors identify a critical limitation: the sparse reward structure of SFT, where the reward is a Dirac delta function, leading to an importance weight that is inversely proportional to the model's probability of generating the correct token. This can result in overly large gradients and unstable training. To address this, DFT reweights the SFT loss by incorporating the probability of each token, effectively normalizing the gradient updates. This approach aims to mitigate the instability caused by the sparse reward structure and improve generalization.
