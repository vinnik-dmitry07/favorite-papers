## Summary:

This paper introduces XBridge, a novel communication protocol designed to facilitate efficient information transfer between heterogeneous large language models (LLMs). The core problem addressed is the entity grounding challenge that arises when aligning continuous representations across models with different vocabularies and architectures. The authors identify 'entity grounding' as a critical issue where continuous bridges, such as latent representations, lose the specific entity identities present in the sender's context. To tackle this, XBridge employs a two-pronged approach: Lexical Anchor Mapping (LAM) and Latent Enrichment Bridge (LEB). LAM translates sender context tokens into the receiver's vocabulary, ensuring that discrete entity identities are preserved and transferred. This is achieved through a deterministic, training-free mapping function that re-tokenizes the sender's context using the receiver's tokenizer. LEB, on the other hand, addresses the issue of rare-token compression collapse, where continuous representations fail to preserve entity identities. LEB leverages gated cross-attention modules that query the sender's hidden states and use the LAM-embedded context to enrich the receiver's representation. This allows the receiver to contextualize the re-tokenized input and recover entity-specific information. The authors evaluate XBridge across three model families: Llama, Qwen, and Mistral, on seven benchmark datasets, demonstrating significant improvements over text-based communication baselines. The experimental results show that XBridge outperforms text-based communication methods on all seven tasks for each model pair, with an average improvement of 21.4% in F1 score. Furthermore, the paper highlights the efficiency of XBridge, showing that it achieves 11x lower latency than text-based methods and requires only 264M trainable parameters, making it a resource-efficient solution. The authors also demonstrate that XBridge can be composed across different models, further enhancing its practical applicability. The paper's findings suggest that XBridge is a promising approach for enabling effective communication between heterogeneous LLMs, overcoming the limitations of traditional text-based methods and opening new avenues for multi-agent collaboration.
