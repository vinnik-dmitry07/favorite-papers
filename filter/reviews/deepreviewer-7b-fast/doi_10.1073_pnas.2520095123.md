## Summary:

The use of both fMRI and behavioral data, along with the inclusion of a patient sample, strengthens the study's conclusions and provides a comprehensive view of the neural basis of logical reasoning. The paper's findings have implications for our understanding of the relationship between language and cognition, suggesting that these two domains, while related, are not necessarily intertwined in the neural processes underlying abstract reasoning.
