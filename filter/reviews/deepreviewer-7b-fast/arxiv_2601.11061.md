## Summary:

This paper presents a compelling investigation into the phenomenon of 'spurious rewards' in reinforcement learning applied to large language models (LLMs), specifically focusing on the Qwen2.5-Math-7B model. The authors identify a unique 'Perplexity Paradox,' where models exhibit improved performance on contaminated datasets despite increased prompt perplexity, indicating a degradation in language modeling capabilities. Through a combination of mechanistic interpretability techniques and empirical analysis, they reveal a specific circuit within the network that enables shortcut retrieval, termed the 'Anchor-Adapter circuit.' This circuit is localized to specific layers, with a functional anchor (MLP) in the middle layers (L18-20) and structural adapters in later layers (L21-22). The authors demonstrate that manipulating this circuit, specifically through scaling MLP keys, allows for bidirectional causal steering of the shortcut retrieval. They also introduce a 'Probe-Gated Shortcut Audit' mechanism to selectively identify and mitigate these shortcut dependencies. The core methodological approach involves a multi-pronged strategy using Path Patching, Logit Lens, Jensen-Shannon Divergence (JSD), Neural Differential Equations (NDEs), and ablation studies. The key empirical findings include the identification of the functional anchor and structural adapters, the demonstration of causal steering of the shortcut, and the validation of the Probe-Gated Shortcut Audit. The overall significance of this work lies in its ability to provide a mechanistic understanding of how spurious rewards can lead to memorization over general reasoning, offering a pathway for identifying and mitigating data contamination in LLMs. This research is a valuable contribution to the field, providing both a deeper understanding of the underlying mechanisms and practical tools for addressing the issue of shortcut learning in LLMs.
