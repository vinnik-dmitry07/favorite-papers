The core motivation stems from the observation that while fine-grained Monte Carlo (MC) reward estimation can theoretically provide more precise policy updates, it often leads to high variance and unstable training, particularly in early reasoning stages. The authors propose a hierarchical approach that decomposes the reasoning process into two distinct phases: an upstream phase and a downstream phase. In the upstream phase, the model generates an intermediate reasoning segment, which is then used as a prefix for the downstream phase. This downstream phase employs MC sampling to refine the reasoning process, aiming to mitigate the high variance associated with fine-grained rewards. A key innovation is the use of a skip connection, where the generated upstream segment is concatenated with the original problem statement, allowing the downstream model to leverage the intermediate reasoning while retaining the ability to explore alternative solution paths.
