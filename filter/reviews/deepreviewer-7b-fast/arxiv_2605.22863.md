## Summary:

This paper introduces Latent Cache Flow (LCF), a novel approach for inter-model communication in large language models (LLMs), aiming to address the limitations of traditional text-based communication and the inefficiencies of existing cache-to-cache (C2C) methods. The core idea behind LCF is to transfer knowledge between models by compressing and transmitting their key-value (KV) caches through a shared, low-dimensional latent space, rather than directly exchanging text tokens. This approach is motivated by the observation that key and value states can be compressed individually and that cross-model representations can be approximately linearly aligned, thus reducing the overhead associated with direct cache exchange. The authors propose two variants: LCF for exact-context sharing and LCF-X for cross-context communication. LCF-X further enhances flexibility by summarizing new information not shared between models. The method involves projecting the KV caches into a low-dimensional latent space, applying a shared latent pipeline, and using layer pruning to remove receiver layers that do not benefit from cache updates. The authors evaluate LCF in both shared-context and cross-context settings, comparing it against text-based transmission (T2T) and C2C baselines. The results indicate that LCF achieves comparable performance to C2C with significantly fewer parameters and reduced latency, while LCF-X demonstrates the ability to transfer information across different contexts. The paper's main contribution lies in its novel approach to inter-model communication, which leverages the properties of KV caches and introduces a compressed, latent space for efficient transfer. The authors also introduce a position-free summary mechanism in LCF-X to handle cross-context scenarios, which is a significant step towards more flexible and adaptable multi-model systems. Overall, the paper presents a promising direction for inter-model communication, offering a potential solution to the limitations of text-based transfer and the inefficiencies of existing cache-based methods. However, several limitations and areas for improvement are identified, which will be discussed in detail in the following sections.
