## Summary:

This paper introduces Subproblem Curriculum Reinforcement Learning (SCRL), a novel approach designed to enhance the training of large language models (LLMs) for complex mathematical reasoning tasks. The core idea behind SCRL is to decompose challenging problems into a sequence of verifiable subproblems, thereby creating a structured learning curriculum. This curriculum guides the model to learn from easier to harder problems, facilitating a smoother learning process. The method begins by generating a set of K verifiable subproblems from a reference solution using an external LLM. These subproblems are then presented to the policy model in a single on-policy rollout. To facilitate fine-grained credit assignment, the authors introduce subproblem-level normalization, which computes advantages for each subproblem independently. This allows the model to learn from partial progress and intermediate rewards, addressing the limitations of standard outcome-based reinforcement learning. The paper also incorporates mixed-group training, which combines both curriculum rollouts and original-problem rollouts to reduce prompt mismatch and improve generalization. Empirically, SCRL demonstrates significant performance gains across multiple mathematical reasoning benchmarks, including OlympiadBench, Minerva, MATH-500, AIME 2024, AIME 2025, AMC, and IMO-Bench. Overall, this work presents a compelling approach to curriculum learning for mathematical reasoning, with strong empirical results and a solid theoretical foundation.
