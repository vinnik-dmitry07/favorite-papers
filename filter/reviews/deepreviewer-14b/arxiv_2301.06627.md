The authors define formal competence as the mastery of linguistic rules and patterns, akin to grammatical correctness, while functional competence encompasses the ability to use language effectively in real-world contexts, requiring integration with broader cognitive skills. They argue that while LLMs demonstrate impressive proficiency in formal aspects of language, particularly in English, they fall short in achieving human-like functional competence. This disparity, they propose, stems from the models' lack of access to the rich non-linguistic cognitive abilities that humans rely on for effective communication.
