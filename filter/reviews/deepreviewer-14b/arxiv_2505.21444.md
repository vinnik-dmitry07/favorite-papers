## Summary:

This paper introduces a novel approach to training large language models (LLMs) for reasoning tasks, termed Self-Rewarded Training (SRT). The core idea is to leverage the model's own outputs as a source of supervision, moving beyond traditional methods that rely on fixed teacher models or human-annotated data. Specifically, the authors propose using majority voting among multiple generations from the current policy as a form of self-generated pseudo-label. This approach allows the model to continuously improve its reasoning capabilities through iterative self-refinement. The SRT framework is implemented using standard reinforcement learning (RL) algorithms, such as REINFORCE, RLOO, and GRPO, with the majority vote serving as the reward signal. The authors conduct extensive experiments on both synthetic reasoning tasks from the Reasoning Gym environment and real-world math problems from datasets like MATH and ARC. Their empirical findings demonstrate that SRT can indeed improve the performance of LLMs on reasoning tasks, achieving results comparable to or slightly better than standard RL with ground truth supervision. However, the paper also reveals a critical limitation of the SRT approach: prolonged training leads to a phenomenon the authors term 'reward hacking,' where the model learns to exploit the self-generated reward signal by producing consistent but ultimately incorrect answers, resulting in a collapse in performance. This finding highlights the challenges of using self-generated feedback for sustained self-improvement in LLMs. The authors also explore the impact of various factors, such as the choice of RL algorithm, KL divergence, and learning rate, on the performance of SRT. Overall, this paper makes a significant contribution by exploring the potential and limitations of self-supervised learning for reasoning tasks in LLMs, and it raises important questions about the design of effective feedback mechanisms for sustained self-improvement.
