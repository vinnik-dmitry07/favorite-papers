## Summary:

This paper introduces Group Sequence Policy Optimization (GSPO), a novel reinforcement learning (RL) algorithm designed for training large language models (LLMs), particularly Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) models. The core innovation of GSPO lies in its sequence-level approach to importance sampling, clipping, rewarding, and optimization, contrasting with the token-level methods used in previous algorithms like Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO). The authors argue that GRPO's token-level importance weights, derived from single samples, introduce high-variance noise that accumulates over long sequences, leading to training instability and potential model collapse. GSPO addresses this by using sequence-level importance ratios based on the likelihood of entire sequences, which they claim aligns better with the sequence-level rewards typically used in RL for LLMs. This approach, according to the authors, provides a more stable and effective learning signal, especially for long sequences. The paper presents empirical results from training the Qwen3 large language model, demonstrating that GSPO achieves superior training stability, efficiency, and performance compared to GRPO. Specifically, GSPO is shown to eliminate the need for complex stabilization strategies like Routing Replay, which is necessary for GRPO when training MoE models.
