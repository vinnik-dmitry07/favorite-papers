## Summary:

This paper introduces a novel decoding strategy called self-consistency, designed to enhance the reasoning capabilities of large language models (LLMs) when applied to complex tasks. The core idea behind self-consistency is to move beyond the traditional greedy decoding approach, which selects the single most probable output, and instead sample a diverse set of reasoning paths from the language model. These sampled paths are then aggregated, typically by taking a majority vote over the final answers, to produce the final prediction. The authors argue that this approach leverages the intuition that a complex reasoning problem often admits multiple different ways of thinking that can lead to the same correct answer.
