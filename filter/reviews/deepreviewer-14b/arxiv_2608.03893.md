## Summary:

This paper introduces a novel approach to cross-model Key-Value (KV) cache transfer, addressing the computational bottleneck of re-prefilling the KV cache when switching between different-sized models within the same family during inference. The core idea is to transfer the KV cache from a source model to a target model, thereby avoiding the costly re-prefilling process. The authors observe that the cross-model KV relationship exhibits a substantial linear structure, which allows them to develop a closed-form ridge regression-based mapping between the KV caches of different models. This mapping is learned using a small calibration dataset and can be applied without requiring gradient-based training. The method involves selecting the top-k most predictive source layers for each target layer, stripping the RoPE encoding from the keys before mapping, and fitting a ridge regression model. The authors demonstrate the effectiveness of their approach on six matched-KV pairs across three model families, showing that the linear mapper retains a significant portion of the receiver's standalone-prefill accuracy. They further show that a nonlinear MLP can recover some of the accuracy lost by the linear approach on more challenging transfer pairs. The proposed method offers a computationally efficient alternative to re-prefilling, with the potential to significantly reduce latency and computational costs in scenarios involving frequent model switching.
