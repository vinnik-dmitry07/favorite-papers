## Summary:

This paper introduces XBridge, a novel communication protocol designed to facilitate interaction between heterogeneous large language models (LLMs). The core problem addressed is the challenge of enabling effective information transfer between LLMs that have been trained independently and thus possess incompatible internal representations, or 'latent spaces.' The authors identify a critical issue in current cross-architecture communication, which they term the 'entity grounding problem.' This problem arises when continuous representations, used to transfer context between models, suffer from a 'rare-token compression collapse,' leading to a loss of crucial entity identity information. To tackle this, XBridge employs a dual-channel approach. First, it uses Lexical Anchor Mapping (LAM) to create discrete entity anchors by mapping the sender's context tokens to the receiver's vocabulary. This process aims to preserve the lexical identity of entities by transferring the original tokens, re-tokenized using the receiver's tokenizer, thus avoiding information loss through compression. Second, XBridge introduces a Latent Enrichment Bridge (LEB), which uses gated cross-attention to allow the receiver to query the sender's hidden states for contextual enrichment. This mechanism enables the receiver to access the sender's contextual understanding without requiring the sender to perform computationally expensive autoregressive decoding. The authors demonstrate the effectiveness of XBridge through extensive experiments across three different LLM families (Llama, Qwen, and Mistral), seven diverse benchmarks, and various communication directions. The results show that XBridge significantly outperforms text-based communication methods, achieving an 11x reduction in latency while maintaining or improving performance. Furthermore, in same-architecture settings, XBridge exceeds the performance of a key-value (KV) sharing baseline on six out of seven tasks. The paper also includes ablation studies that highlight the importance of both LAM and LEB components, showing that removing either component leads to a significant drop in performance. The authors emphasize that XBridge is a lightweight solution, adding only a small number of trainable parameters (264M) and requiring minimal training time. Overall, this work presents a significant step towards enabling efficient and effective communication between heterogeneous LLMs, addressing a critical bottleneck in the development of multi-agent systems.
